Build tonight's plan.

One screen decides how the night ends — and you can change your mind at any point.

Tonight turns a power action into a session. Pick how long is left, pick what the PC does when the time comes — shutdown, sleep, restart, hibernate, or log out — and either use a Night Mode or start from scratch.

  • One-tap presets: a 45-minute sleep, a 30-minute wind-down, a two-hour shutdown.
  • Night Modes give sessions preset endings — a movie wind-down, a full shutdown, a quiet-house night — or save your own Profile for quick reuse.
  • Set a clock-time target instead of a duration when the clock matters more.
  • While it runs: current phase, remaining time, then pause, snooze, or cancel — always available.

Your phone becomes the bedside remote.

The Android companion runs over your local network — pair it once, then steer Tonight from bed.

Start the session, change the duration, switch the PC action, pause, snooze, or cancel — without getting back out of bed. The companion shows live remaining time, reconnects across LAN changes, and keeps working while the desktop app hides in the tray.

  • Pairing is a one-time device confirmation — no cloud account, no internet hop.
  • Change the Night Mode from the phone and the desktop follows.
  • The desktop can stay hidden in the tray; the session keeps running and stays reachable.

The companion controls Tonight. It does not control smart lights, Wi-Fi Guard, or the proof ledger — those stay on the desktop in this build.

Lights Out at night. First Light in the morning.

The night doesn't end when the PC goes to sleep — Lights Out schedules the morning too.

First Light is a real wake alarm. Set a time in the app, and Lights Out registers it with Windows Task Scheduler so the PC can wake from sleep on its own. When it wakes, the app runs a rising First Light sequence — a gradual, warm start instead of a cold alert.

  • On wake, the session shows a morning briefing drawn from your live calendar and sleep-debt state.
  • Smart lights configured for the morning can join the sunrise with a bounded brightness transition.
  • The one-shot wake task removes itself after it fires.

Two supporting systems round out the routine: a Bedtime Reminder that nudges you before bed (on by default, 15 minutes before), and Autopilot Bedtime, which learns your pattern and offers to start the session — off by default until you turn it on.

Let the room wind down with you.

Compatible lights can dim and warm as the bedtime session progresses — attached to the session, not on their own schedules.

Smart Lights join the session lifecycle. As the timer runs, configured lights can dim toward a warm color temperature where the fixture supports it, and switch off when the session ends — part of the same wind-down you started on the PC.

  • In-app setup exposes Philips Hue and HTTP webhook integrations today.
  • Smart Lights only arm after a real successful test connection, and any configuration change disarms them until they pass again.
  • Ritual and wind-down steps can trigger light actions as part of the sequence.

Independent per-light schedules — “Bedroom lights off at 11:00 PM, kitchen at 11:30” — are not part of this build. Light behavior is tied to the PC session tonight.

Make the network part of the wind-down.Experimental

Wi-Fi Guard can put the network to bed with you — through supported router and webhook integrations.

Wi-Fi Guard restricts internet access as part of the bedtime flow — one more reason to actually log off. It supports TP-Link, ASUS, and Netgear routers, custom webhooks, and a local Wi-Fi-adapter fallback, and it restores access when the session ends or is cancelled.

  • It is labelled Experimental inside the app: router paths are best-effort and model-dependent.
  • It refuses to arm without a completed provider setup — an unconfigured guard cannot be switched on.
  • No independent per-device internet schedules in this build.

Build a routine, not just a countdown.

Lights Out can start from more than a button — several real scheduling systems give the night a shape.

The Schedule screen is where the week gets predictable. It brings together everything that makes a routine stick:

  • One-off and recurring shutdown rules — “at this time, on these days.”
  • A clock-time target alongside the duration timer, and a clock display for the night.
  • Calendar-based behavior: live calendar events feed session decisions and the First Light morning briefing.
  • Content Blocker schedules: blocking windows that restrict distracting sites during wind-down, independently of the timer.

One honest boundary: these systems are independently scheduled, not one saved whole-room timeline. “Kitchen lights dim at 11:00, Wi-Fi off at 11:30, PC sleeps at midnight” as a single plan is future direction — not current copy.

Know what actually happened.

Automated power actions shouldn't leave you guessing. The Proof screen records the session, and the receipt stays on your machine.

Every session produces a real record: what ran, which action, the outcome — including when you cancelled or changed course. Session history and a full ledger stay in the app, and Morning Proof surfaces the last run's results the next morning.

  • Session History with per-run outcome and status.
  • A full ledger for longer-term review.
  • Morning Proof: the results of last night, shown when you're back at the machine.
  • Completion streaks and a routine consistency score, tracked per night.

Automation you can still stop.

The system has the power; you keep the controls.

Everything that touches the machine is designed to be interruptible. Before anything runs you confirm it; while it runs you can always stop it.

  • Final confirmation before the session starts
  • Pause, snooze, and cancel from the app, the tray, or the phone
  • Emergency cancel anywhere: Ctrl+Shift+X global shortcut
  • Dry Run mode — rehearse the full flow without touching the system
  • A 2-minute default grace period before the action (configurable)
  • Force shutdown only when you explicitly opt in
  • Local-only operation — no cloud, no account, no telemetry
  • Status and proof after every run, including cancellations
