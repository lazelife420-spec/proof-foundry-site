Quick definitions

Cover — a short-term credit line KOHO extends against the next paycheque. It can only be spent on the KOHO card itself; it cannot be e-transferred to a bank account or another person.
Credit Building — a KOHO product that locks part of each paycheque away as collateral to help build credit history. That money is inaccessible until KOHO releases it — and it isn't a one-time lock: it has to be reapplied for every single month, then there's at least a 3-day wait before any released funds become available.

5This Isn't a Spending Problem

No amount of budgeting changes this before July 21st. The moment the paycheque lands, KOHO automatically sweeps ~$969 for Cover repayment and Credit Building — before any spending decision is made. That happens whether every discretionary dollar is cut to zero or not.

The $600 that comes back as "Cover" isn't optional to touch — it's the only form the money takes. It can't sit unused as savings; it can't be e-transferred to cover rent or debt. It's card credit by design, and it's the same $600 that gets clawed back next payday no matter how carefully it's spent.

Credit Building adds its own timing trap on top of that: it isn't a one-time lock either. It has to be reapplied for every single month, and even once it's approved there's at least a 3-day wait before any released funds actually become available. That delay is set by KOHO's process, not by planning or budgeting — there's no way to speed it up.

In other words: this cycle isn't the result of overspending this month. It's how the product is structured to repay itself. The only thing that changes it is the one move already in motion — let the July 21st sweep happen and don't re-borrow the Cover. Until that day, the math is fixed regardless of behaviour.
